# ApplyFlow — Product Direction v0.2 (Skills-First Discovery)

**Updated:** November 8, 2026  
**Status:** Clickable/editable Figma concept prototype; not a coded or live internship-discovery service.  
**Figma (mobile discovery):** https://www.figma.com/design/AKLLIxOhHKBs8qdTUhKX2t?node-id=8-136  
**Figma (desktop discovery):** https://www.figma.com/design/AKLLIxOhHKBs8qdTUhKX2t?node-id=9-87

## Product hypothesis

Students can miss relevant internships when they search only familiar job titles or filter exclusively by major. Even after finding unfamiliar roles, they may struggle to connect their coursework or projects to responsibilities and distinguish hard eligibility requirements from preferred qualifications.

**Proposed positioning:** Discover internships your major doesn't tell you to search for.

ApplyFlow should support informed decisions, not tell students they are guaranteed to qualify.

## Discovery signals from five supplied interviews

Themes recurring across participant narratives:

- Existing spreadsheets often work adequately for logging applications; migration is a major adoption barrier.
- Students encounter unexpected relevant role families through peers rather than regular search queries.
- Transferable skill evidence and required-vs-preferred qualification distinctions are more useful than an opaque match score.
- Duplicate listings and interview coordination remain secondary opportunities, but not primary MVP differentiators.
- Some users will not pay, upload a resume, or create an account before receiving value.

The interview text was supplied by the project owner. Participant counts and details should be reconciled and consent confirmed before publishing attributable or precise personal research records.

## Initial curated concept test (five participants)

Each participant was shown five **manually selected** illustrative real-world role opportunities in the supplied test narrative (25 total). Reported outcomes:

- 15/25 opportunities were described as relevant and previously unfamiliar/overlooked (participant counts: 3, 3, 4, 2, 3).
- Reviewing five opportunities took approximately 5.8 minutes on average, based on participant reports.
- 10 job posting visits, reported across participants.
- Five participants reported applying to one opportunity each following the test.

**Evidence limits:** These are participant-reported outcomes from a small, non-random, manually curated exercise—not outcomes delivered by the Figma prototype, proof of recommendation accuracy, or causal evidence of improved application performance. Do not claim an algorithm achieved these outcomes, or that all applications are independently verified. Baseline comparisons are informal, not controlled.

## v0.2 Figma screens

1. **Skills & Experience:** A project story, transferable-skill chips, interests, and privacy-first onboarding.
2. **Discover Beyond Your Major:** Role families and illustrative internship opportunities with concise reasoning.
3. **Understand Your Fit:** Experience-to-responsibility mapping, required vs preferred criteria, and questions to verify.
4. **Saved Opportunities:** Role shortlist with optional user-defined next steps and no spreadsheet migration.
5. **Desktop Discover:** Responsive concept for a larger-screen exploration workflow.

v0.1 is retained on its own page in the same Figma file as an archive of the original tracking-first hypothesis.

## Ground rules for the UI

- Do not label simulated job listings as active or real; **all employer names and role details in the Figma mockups are demo data**.
- Do not invent or scrape live openings; obtain actual postings through permitted sources later.
- Do not infer hard requirements from a job's title or field. Display original employer wording and cite its posting when data integration exists.
- Do not use unjustified candidate-match percentages, hiring probability, or "guaranteed eligible" statements.
- No mandatory resume upload, OAuth connections, or migration in the initial demo.
- No paid APIs or automated applying in the first $0-budget MVP.
- Any later automatic explanations need evaluation for omissions, overstatement, and eligibility errors.

## Highest-priority follow-up tests

Test the revised concept with ~10 new students. Track (a) truly new and relevant opportunities vs their normal searches, (b) eligibility interpretation accuracy, (c) time to decision on comparable roles, (d) posting visits and completed applications, and (e) return use over 2–4 weeks. Use original posting links and a documented selection procedure. Include cases with hard exclusion criteria and cases where evidence is ambiguous.

**Build decision:** Do not code a complex job aggregator until a low-cost, repeatable way of sourcing genuinely fresh relevant opportunities is proven. A manually curated catalog with clear reasoning is an appropriate first test.
